Select one of the scenarios below and design a survey to meet the need(s) outlined in the prompt.

1.	In two to three sentences, describe the purpose of your survey
The survey aims to find out what issues and leader qualities matter most to Canadian voters one month before the federal election, and to measure how those priorities affect vote intention for our party. 2.	Describe your target population, sampling frame, sampling units, and overall sampling strategy.

Target population - All Canadian citizens aged 18 and over who are likely to vote in the upcoming federal election

Sampling frame - A combined, up-to-date listing of contact sources covering the target population: a probability-based online panel and telephone lists built from provincial voter contact files and Random Digit Dialing (RDD) for landlines and mobile phones. Where available, use commercial voter files that include likely-voter indicators; supplement with RDD/phone to reach underrepresented groups.

Sampling unit - Individual eligible voters (one respondent per household). If a household is reached, the sampling protocol specifies selecting the respondent whose birthday is next or another random within-household selection method.

Overall sampling strategy (practical, representativeness + battleground focus)

Use a stratified multistage approach. Stratify by province/territory and within provinces by urban vs. rural (or by major metropolitan regions vs. other).

Within strata, draw a probability-based sample of individuals using a mix of voter files and RDD to maximize coverage.

Oversample in key battleground ridings/regions where the race is close, to get reliable local estimates (then weight back to national proportions).

Set quotas or post-stratification weights for age, gender, and education to match known population benchmarks (Census or voter file distributions) and to correct for differential nonresponse.

Screen and include only likely voters (use a turnout likelihood question) and target a final effective sample size of about 1,500–2,000 likely voters nationally (larger in tight regions if budget allows) so margins of error are acceptable for national and some subgroups.

### Part B - Survey Evaluation:

For the **Canadian General Social Survey on Giving, Volunteering, and Participating, 2018 (cycle 33)**, conducted by Statistics Canada find any and all available documentation for the data gathered and identify and describe the survey features indicated below.

1. Sample type - Probability-based cross-sectional household survey
2. Sample size - Field/sample contacts were large (~50,000 units in the sampling frame)
4. Sampling frame - Persons aged 18 years and older living in private dwellings in the ten provinces of Canada.
5. Survey mode(s) - Telephone numbers and dwelling records linked to the Statistics Canada dwelling frame and administrative sources.
6. Timeline - Questions on volunteering and donations referred to the 12 months preceding the interview.
7. Response rate - Overall response rates were in the low-40% range
8. Weights - Person-level survey weights are provided on the microdata
9. Data processing - Responses were captured via the electronic questionnaire system (EQ/iEQ). Processing included automated validation and edit checks, coding, derivation of variables, weighting, and creation of the Public Use Microdata File (PUMF) with documentation.
10. Cleaning, imputation, etc - Item nonresponse was handled using donor imputation (nearest donor imputation methods) for many items, including income and some volunteering/donation items.

Administrative linkage: Personal and family income data were supplemented by linkage to tax files for respondents who consented (high linkage coverage), improving income quality.

Subsampling handling: The weighting and estimation procedures account for the rejective/subsampling scheme so that published estimates represent the target population
11. Sources of error - Sampling error (complex design — must use bootstrap replicate weights for SEs).

Nonresponse error (moderate response rates can produce bias even after weighting).

Coverage error (exclusion of institutional residents and possible undercoverage of households without phone/address linkage).

Measurement error (recall bias for hours/donations, social desirability).

Processing/imputation error (donor imputation assumptions and linkage non-consent impacts). 


12. Limitations, known biases, etc - Lower response rates increase reliance on weighting and administrative linkage to adjust for nonresponse; residual bias may remain.

The rejective/subsampling design (long vs short form) means some items are collected for all respondents and some only for subsamples — analysts must use appropriate weights and design variables.

Coverage gaps (territories/institutions) and recall/social desirability biases (self-reported volunteering, hours, donations) limit certain inferences at fine subpopulations.

Comparability issues: GSS 2018 introduced collection mode changes (EQ/web emphasis) and questionnaire changes vs earlier cycles, so some time comparisons may not be straightforward.

## Part B - Survey Evaluation:

Identify and describe survey features:

Sample Type:
Probability-based cross-sectional household survey.

Sample Size:
Approximately 16,149 respondents.

Target Population:
Persons aged 15 years and older living in private dwellings in the ten provinces of Canada (excludes institutional residents).

Sampling Frame:
Telephone numbers (landline and mobile) linked to dwelling addresses and other administrative sources to select households.

Sampling Units:
Individual persons within selected households.

Observational Units:
Respondents who completed the survey and provided information on giving, volunteering, and participating.

Survey Mode(s):
Mixed-mode: web-based self-administered questionnaire and computer-assisted telephone interviewing (CATI).

Timeline:
Data collection occurred from September to December 2018; reference period for key measures was the previous 12 months.

Response Rate:
Varies by source; roughly around 40% (exact rate depends on methodology and region).

Weights:
Person-level weights adjust for selection probability, nonresponse, and calibration to population benchmarks. Bootstrap replicate weights are provided for variance estimation.

Data Processing / Cleaning / Imputation:
Includes data validation, editing, imputation of missing values (e.g., donation amounts), and linkage to administrative data where available.

Sources of Error:
Sampling error (complex design), nonresponse bias, coverage error (exclusions of institutions or territories), measurement error (self-reported data), and processing/imputation errors.

Limitations / Known Biases:
Mode changes (web vs CATI) and questionnaire updates in 2018 limit comparability with earlier cycles. Some population segments may be underrepresented; nonresponse bias cannot be fully eliminated.

Documentation / Links:

Public Use Microdata File & User Guide: GSS 2018 PUMF Documentation

Survey methodology and details: Statistics Canada IMDB – GSS GVP
